# Two-year thesis

Adaptive agents will be judged less by how quickly they produce another plan and more by whether they preserve commitments while changing it.

The core production artifact will be a revision record: the observation that contradicted an assumption, the work already completed, the constraints that remained fixed, and the new effects that became permissible. A fluent explanation without that record will be insufficient.

Planning and execution will become separate services. Models will propose alternatives, while durable workflows decide whether a proposal fits the current world, remaining budget and irreversible history. Replanning will consume an explicit allowance. Systems will pause when observations conflict, available alternatives are unsafe, or repeated revision becomes more expensive than human review.

Benchmarks will compare adaptive agents against frozen plans on identical event streams. They will measure completed objectives, avoidable loss, compensation, wasted calls and constraint violations, rather than rewarding every change of mind.

The valuable systems will adapt their beliefs faster than they expand their authority. That distinction makes autonomy recoverable, inspectable and economically useful.
